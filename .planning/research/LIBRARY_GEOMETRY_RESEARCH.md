## Scope per topic

**Analytical Geometry** (Term 2): distance formula, midpoint formula,
gradient formula, equations of lines through given points, parallel (equal
gradient) and perpendicular (negative reciprocal gradient) conditions. Out of
scope (inferred): circles in the coordinate plane, locus problems, coordinate
proof of quadrilateral properties — Grade 11+.

**Euclidean Geometry** (Term 4): parallel-line angle relationships
(corresponding/alternate/co-interior), triangle angle sum, triangle types and
properties, quadrilateral family properties (parallelogram/rectangle/rhombus/
square/kite/trapezium), basic congruency/similarity conditions (SSS, SAS, RHS
in basic form), midpoint theorem (proved or at least the parallel part
proved, length relation as corollary), semi-formal statement-reason proof
structure. Out of scope (inferred): full circle geometry theorems, cyclic
quadrilaterals, tangents, compound-angle riders — Grade 11+.

**Measurement** (Term 4): surface area and volume of prisms, cylinders,
spheres, cones, pyramids (formulas given, not derived), composite shapes,
unit conversion. Out of scope (inferred): composite-solid derivations,
calculus-based measurement — Grade 11/12.

## Methodology notes (Prompt A)

Geometry keeps the existing lesson spine (explicit teaching → worked
examples → fading practice → mastery gating → misconception feedback) but
needs real additions:

- **Proof scaffolding**: "Given → Prove" structure, statement-reason pairing
  (semi-formal two-column at Grade 10, not full Grade 11/12 rigidity).
  Misconceptions to target: circular reasoning, assuming a diagram is to
  scale/has unstated right angles, incorrect or missing reasons, using a
  property in the wrong logical direction (sufficient vs. necessary
  conditions), over-algebraic "solve the proof" mindset.
- **Diagram discipline**: static, clearly-marked diagrams (tick marks for
  equal sides/angles, parallel-line arrows) for proof/rider practice, paired
  with explicit "don't assume from the drawing" warnings early on. Dynamic
  manipulation (dragging points) is "close to essential" for concept-building
  per the research, but static diagrams are what proof practice actually
  needs.
- **Analytical Geometry as a hybrid**: extends the existing FunctionGraph-
  style Cartesian-plane approach, not a wholesale new visual system — add
  triangle/segment overlays (right-angle-triangle visualization for distance,
  segment+midpoint highlighting) on top of point/line plotting.
- **Measurement**: needs strong diagram-interpretation and unit-consistency
  support (surface-area-vs-volume confusion, radius-vs-diameter
  misreadings, unit conversion errors) — feedback should point at the
  specific diagram element misused, not just state "wrong."

**Priority flag from the research**: Euclidean Geometry proofs/riders are
the highest-priority topic for a dedicated topic-specific research pass
(same treatment as Quadratic/Simultaneous Equations got in Algebra) —
described as "highly misconception-prone." Measurement is a second-tier
candidate for a lighter dedicated pass. Analytical Geometry can likely reuse
the existing graph-topic methodology with targeted misconception handling,
no dedicated pass needed.
