# Euclidean Geometry Proofs — Dedicated Topic Research

**Source:** Perplexity deep-research pass, human-run, 2026-07-23. Dedicated
topic-specific pass per the priority flag in
`LIBRARY_GEOMETRY_RESEARCH.md` — Euclidean proofs are the highest-priority
Geometry topic for this treatment. Grounded in Grade 10 "acceptable reasons"
documents, teacher support materials (The Answer Series, exam memos), and
Siyavula — the human flagged CAPS itself doesn't print a reason bank or
explicit prove-vs-accept split, so this is inferred from widely-used Grade 10
practice, not verbatim CAPS text.

## Scope: proved vs. accepted, and proof formality

**Proved at Grade 10** (statement-reason chains): straight-line/point angle
facts, vertically opposite angles, parallel-line angle relationships
(alternate/corresponding/co-interior) AND their converses, triangle angle
sum, exterior angle theorem, isosceles triangle angle/side properties,
Pythagoras and its converse, congruency criteria (SSS/SAS/AAS-ASA/RHS),
basic similarity (equiangular triangles → proportional sides), the midpoint
theorem and its converse, parallelogram properties and their converses
(classification).

**Accepted/applied without re-proof** (established earlier, used as given):
right angle = 90°, supplementary/complementary definitions — though sources
vary on exactly how much is re-proved vs. assumed at Grade 10.

**Proof formality**: semi-formal two-column (Statement | Reason) is the
practical norm most exam memos and teacher materials use — this is what was
built. Narrative explanation is fine for teaching, but learner output should
be statement-reason pairs.

## Confirmed teaching sequence (matches what was built)

1. Lines and angles (straight line, point/revolution, vertically opposite,
   parallel-line relationships + converses) — foundation for everything else.
2. Triangles (angle sum, exterior angle, isosceles, Pythagoras, congruency
   SSS/SAS/AAS-ASA/RHS, basic similarity).
3. Quadrilaterals (parallelogram family properties, classification via
   converses).
4. Midpoint theorem as capstone, connecting triangles/quadrilaterals/
   coordinate geometry.

## Misconceptions used (condensed per topic — see each topic file for the
full per-topic list; all four topics deliberately target circular reasoning,
diagram-scale assumptions, and incorrect/missing reasons as recurring
threads, plus topic-specific ones: angle-relationship mislabelling and
missing-parallel-condition for lines/angles; congruency-criterion
misapplication and congruency/similarity confusion for triangles;
appearance-based misclassification and using-a-property-before-proving-it
for quadrilaterals; "definition not theorem" and missing-true-midpoint
condition for the midpoint theorem.

## Assessment/practice item shape used

Fill-in-the-blank proofs with 1-2 blanks in early practice (heavily
scaffolded, most statements/reasons given), building to independent practice
items with the reason bank as multiple-choice options rather than free
recall — matches the research's "select the correct reason" interaction
design and its guidance on blank-count/scaffolding progression.
