Project Title: Prospect: A Unified, Explainable Digital Platform for Academic Management and Early Intervention in South African Schools


ABSTRACT

Purpose

South African schools, particularly those with large classes and limited resources, often have no systematic way to identify precisely which part of a topic a student had not understood, or to notice a student falling behind before end-of-term results made it obvious. This project investigated whether an affordable, explainable software platform could close both gaps by adapting two established but locally untested approaches: mastery-based diagnostic testing and rule-based early-warning detection of at-risk students.

Method

A web-based school platform, Prospect, was designed and built for four user roles — administrators, teachers, students, and parents — sharing a single underlying database of marks, attendance, and behaviour records. Two features were developed as the focus of this investigation: a Topic Test engine that measured whether a student had genuinely mastered a topic, rather than guessed correctly, using a consistency-based scoring approach; and an At-Risk Intervention engine that flagged struggling students using multiple attendance, behaviour, and course-performance indicators, always displaying the specific reasoning behind each flag. Both engines were tested against constructed sample datasets representing a term's worth of student activity across multiple classes and subjects, deliberately including students with declining, inconsistent, and consistently low performance.
